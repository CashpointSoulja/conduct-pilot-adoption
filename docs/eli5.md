# ELI5

Imagine a school wants to change one rule: every lunch order from the sports team gets an extra
50p for delivery. That sounds like one change, but the till, the receipt printer, the van driver's
list and the money report all need to know about it.

This tool is my checklist for that kind of change:

1. Write down what we are trying to prove and how things are today. If nobody knows how long
   things take today, I write "Unknown", not a guess.
2. Draw lines from the rule to everything it touches, and write who looks after each thing.
3. If something is broken, someone's name goes next to it before we move on.
4. Run the tests. If a test fails, or the test result is more than a day old, we stop and say who
   fixes it.
5. A grown-up signs off on this exact version. If anything changes after they sign, they sign again.
6. Pretend to switch it on (nothing real is switched on).
7. Count who actually started using it, say who we left out of the count and why, and check the
   sports team and the chess club separately in case one hides the other.
8. Write it up for the person who looks after the school next, plus a list of what we know for sure
   and what we only suspect.

Everything in it is made up: the company, the people and the numbers.
